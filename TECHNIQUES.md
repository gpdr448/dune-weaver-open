# Techniques

Tested on this table. Spacings are in rho units (1 rho ≈ 136 mm radially);
for sideways spacing use (rho + 0.14)·Δθ — see MACHINE.md.

## Spacing — the chisel law

| gap | result |
|---|---|
| ≤ 0.035 | two passes fuse into one wide trough |
| 0.045 – 0.065 | a thin knife-edge ridge survives; the earlier pass partly silts |
| ≥ 0.075 | two clean troughs with a real ridge between |
| ≥ 0.105 | a broad ridge that keeps the grain's texture on top |

A raster finer than the ball (e.g. a spiral at 0.02) does not go flat: it leaves
fine ridges at its own pitch, like a fingerprint.

## Order is visible

- **The last stroke wins.** Its berms silt earlier neighbours; at a crossing the
  later line reads as passing *over* the earlier one.
- **Retrace to restore.** Retracing the earlier line back through a crossing
  puts it on top again.
- **Cable.** A looped line (e.g. a prolate cycloid) reads as twisted rope because
  every crossing is over/under by order; drawing it the other direction twists
  it the other way. Knotwork follows from planned retraces.
- Below ≈20° two crossing lines merge into one long lens and order stops reading.

## Marks

- **Bead scale** (single circles): r 0.02 a dimple · 0.035 a cup with a bead ·
  0.05 a ring with a raised boss · 0.07 a ring round a mound · 0.10 a ring round
  a flat island.
- **Fingerprint vs dish:** fill a small disc with a one-way spiral (< 0.035 pitch)
  for concentric ridges, or spiral in and back out half a turn out of phase for
  a smooth dish that reads bright.
- **Bold = wider, not deeper.** Retracing the same line changes nothing (the ball
  already reaches the floor). Retrace offset 0.012–0.025 for a wider, bolder line.
- **Corners** of 90° and 60° round off to the trough's radius; 30° and sharper
  close into a slot near the point.
- **Ticks and reversals** end in clean rounded caps.

## Working with the ground

- **Grain.** Lines across the clear's rings leave toothed, comb-like berms; lines
  along them leave smooth berms. The strip between two nearby cross-grain cuts
  becomes a ladder of rungs.
- **The eye.** Rasters crossing the middle part around the central mound — use it
  as a pupil, a knot, a seam.
- **Register void.** Leaving a region untouched lets the grain show as its own
  texture; an untouched inner disc makes outer work read suspended.

## Composition methods that have worked

- **Phase-locked ornament:** an integer motif count per revolution keeps repeats
  aligned radially so ornament never crowds the turn spacing.
- **Offset retrograde:** retrace a line backwards at a small offset — a second
  voice, or a bolder line.
- **Boustrophedon raster:** back-and-forth lines with small connectors; connectors
  along the rim make a decorative border.
- **Convergent fan:** lines from the rim to a point fuse at the root and separate
  outward — reads as pressure toward a point.
- **Two-pitch spirals:** two spirals sharing start and end but differing in turn
  count cross once per turn of difference.
- **Graded motifs:** motifs shrinking and spacing out read as a fade, no lift needed.
- **Density by angle:** tightening a spiral's pitch on one side reads as shadow or
  warmth on that side.
- **Spiral-in tail:** end with a short spiral toward the centre to park the ball.
- **Relief lines (weak):** the ridge between two passes 0.11 apart can be the
  figure, but under overhead light it reads as two grooves. Untested in low light.
